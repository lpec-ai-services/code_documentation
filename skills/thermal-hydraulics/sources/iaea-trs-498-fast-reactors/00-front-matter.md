# FOREWORD

As the world strives to meet the increasing demand for energy while also
reducing its carbon footprint, it is important to explore alternative sources of
energy. One such alternative is fast reactors, which offer a promising means
to achieve energy security while contributing to the transition to a net zero
emissions. Fast reactors are designed to operate at higher temperatures than
conventional reactors and, as a result, they are more efficient and generate less
nuclear waste.
The thermal hydraulics of fast reactors plays a crucial role in ensuring
their safe and reliable operation. Understanding the behaviour of coolant fluids,
such as liquid sodium or lead, in fast reactor cores is essential to predicting the
reactor’s thermal performance and preventing potential accidents.
The IAEA offers its Member States a wide spectrum of education
and training activities and provides scientific and technical publications
including Safety Standards, technical guidance, conference proceedings and
scientific reports.
This publication reviews the state of the art in the thermal hydraulics of
fast reactors, summarizing the latest research in this field. It covers topics such
as coolant flow, heat transfer and thermal hydraulic instabilities and discusses
the challenges and opportunities in the modelling and simulation of fast reactors.
The review also highlights the importance of international collaboration and
knowledge sharing in advancing the development of fast reactors.
The IAEA thanks all those who contributed to the drafting and review
of this publication. The contributions of the participants in the Technical
Meeting on State-of-the-Art Thermal Hydraulics of Fast Reactors are gratefully
acknowledged. The IAEA officers responsible for this publication were
V. Kriventsev and N. Morelová of the Division of Nuclear Power.
