# 1. INTRODUCTION

## 1.1. BACKGROUND

The IAEA convened the Technical Meeting on Methods and Codes for
Calculations of Thermohydraulic Parameters for Fuel, Absorber Pins and
Assemblies of Liquid Metal Cooled Fast Reactors (LMFRs) with Traditional
and Burner Cores in Obninsk, Russian Federation, from 27 to 31 July 1998.
The meeting was organized as a forum for experts from Member States with
fast reactor programmes to collectively review the status of and international
progress made in the core thermohydraulics of LMFRs. More than 30 papers
presented during the meeting were published in 2000 in IAEA-TECDOC-1157,
LMFR Core Thermohydraulics: Status and Prospects [1], providing insights into
the status of worldwide research activities in the area of the thermal hydraulics of
reactor cores cooled by liquid metals, along with the challenges to be addressed
by future research.
Two decades later, there had been significant developments in the field of
fast reactor thermal hydraulics, but the IAEA had not undertaken any studies to
specifically review and understand the state of the art since the last millennium.
Therefore, based on the advice of the Technical Working Group on Fast Reactors,
the IAEA organized the Technical Meeting on State-of-the-art Thermal Hydraulics
of Fast Reactors in September 2022. This event, hosted by the Italian National
Agency for New Technologies, Energy and Sustainable Economic Development
(ENEA), brought together participants from various Member States actively
engaged in or interested in the field of fast reactor thermal hydraulics [2]. The
event reviewed the current status of and international progress made in the field
of fast reactor thermal hydraulics.
The Technical Meeting on State-of-the-art Thermal Hydraulics of Fast
Reactors was open for papers on the thermal hydraulics of all coolants currently
considered for Generation IV fast reactors, as well as for advanced accelerator
driven systems (liquid metals, molten salts, inert gases, supercritical water) at
different scales (local, fuel assembly, core, integral system, containment) under
normal, transient or accident conditions. The Technical Meeting Programme
Committee, in collaboration with the session chairs, has identified and selected
specific contributions for publication. These selected works have been featured
in a special issue of the journal Nuclear Engineering and Design on state of the
art thermal hydraulics of fast reactors [3], dedicated to this IAEA event.
This publication provides a state of the art review of the thermal hydraulics
of fast reactors and discusses the latest innovations and technological challenges

related to the thermal hydraulics of fast reactors and advanced accelerator driven
systems. It is intended to support research and development (R&D), as well as
education and training in nuclear science and technology, on the basis of the
papers presented during the technical meeting.
Most of the material presented in this publication relates to the thermal
hydraulics of LMFRs, reflecting the current status of fast reactor technology. All
past and currently operating fast reactors are cooled by liquid sodium, about half
of the new innovative designs use heavy liquid metals, and only a few emerging
concepts propose molten salt cooled fast reactors (MSFRs) or gas cooled fast
reactors (GFRs).

## 1.2. OBJECTIVES

The objectives of this Technical Reports Series (TRS) publication on
the state of the art in the thermal hydraulics of fast reactors are to (a) present
and discuss the current status of R&D in the field of thermal hydraulics for
Generation IV fast reactors and advanced accelerator driven systems; (b) serve
as a future reference book for experts in the field; (c) summarize the work
presented by the participants in the Technical Meeting on State-of-the-art
Thermal Hydraulics of Fast Reactors; (d) promote and facilitate the exchange
of information within the scope of the technical meeting and its related topics
at the national and international levels; and (e) discuss and identify R&D needs
and gaps to assess the future requirements of the field of fast reactor thermal
hydraulics, which could eventually lead to efforts being concentrated in key areas.

## 1.3. SCOPE

This TRS publication covers topics on fundamental thermal hydraulics
(advances and challenges), computational modelling and simulation, the thermal
hydraulics of accidents and transients, experimental thermal hydraulics, and
legacy reactor data, as well as the need for experimental validation.
It presents reviews of papers presented at the IAEA Technical Meeting on
State-of-the-art Thermal Hydraulics of Fast Reactors (26–30 September 2022,
ENEA, Italy) [2]. All papers mentioned in this publication have been peer
reviewed and discussed at the above technical meeting. The overall scope of the
publication has been developed to support training, workshops and education
programmes in the IAEA Member States. This publication is intended to be
useful for stakeholders in all Member States involved in or interested in the
research, development and/or deployment of fast reactor technologies, including

## 1.4. STRUCTURE

This publication consists of 11 sections.
After a general introduction (Section 1), a brief overview of the thermal
hydraulics of fast reactors (Section 2) summarizes the latest global developments
and the benefits and drawbacks of various fast reactor types and coolants. This
section is followed by insights into the fundamental thermal hydraulics of fast
reactors (Section 3), focusing on topics such as heat transfer and friction factor
correlations and turbulent heat and mass transfer phenomena.
Section 4 focuses on test facilities and experimental thermal hydraulics, as
well as measurement techniques and instrumentation.
System thermal hydraulics, subchannel codes, engineering computational
fluid dynamics (CFD) and their application to safety review are discussed in
Section 5 on computational modelling and simulation.
Operational transients, design basis accidents (DBAs), decay heat removal
and severe accidents are discussed in Section 6, which covers the thermal
hydraulics of accidents and transients.
Code coupling and multiphysics, as well as code platforms under
development, are reviewed in Section 7 on multiscale and multiphysics modelling.
Verification, validation and uncertainty analysis of computer codes used in
liquid metal fast reactor thermal hydraulics are discussed in Section 8. Lessons
learned from international benchmarks and legacy reactor data and the need for
experimental validation are highlighted in Section 9.
Section 10 discusses modern thermal hydraulics tools in licensing.
The final section (Section 11) presents conclusions.
